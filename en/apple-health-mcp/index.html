What Is MCP—and Why Use It Instead of Uploading export.zip?

The Model Context Protocol (MCP) is an open standard that lets AI assistants invoke structured local tools instead of forcing you to paste giant files into a chat box. Rather than stuffing years of raw Apple Health XML into a single prompt, your AI client starts light and fetches specific slices—like last night’s sleep stages or a 30-day HRV series—only when your question calls for them.

MCP Tools Reference (Coming in HealthDock 1.3)

Behind the scenes, HealthDock’s Mac archive stores ordinary files you own—context.md, daily/YYYY/YYYY-MM-DD.json (where an omitted field means no reading was recorded that day, never 0), archive/, routes/*.json, ecg/*.json, journal/, and data dictionaries (CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, DATA.md)—as covered in our Apple Health on Mac guide. When wearables overlap (such as wearing both an Apple Watch and a smart ring), sleep is resolved to one primary source per night, adjustable in Settings.

Tool Name Access What It Does
get_health_context Read-only Returns your 7-day, 30-day, and 90-day summary compared against personal baselines.
query_daily_range Read-only Fetches daily summaries across a start and end date range.
get_day_detail Read-only Returns granular single-day details, such as peak heart rate and the time it occurred.
get_metric_series Read-only Pulls a date-indexed time series for an individual health metric.
get_sleep_stages Read-only Returns deep, REM, core, and awakening stage details for a specific night.
list_workouts Read-only Lists recorded workouts with duration and activity metadata.
list_events / list_goals Read-only Reads logged personal life events and active habit goals from your local archive.

Privacy Boundary: Local MCP Server vs. Your Chosen AI Provider

HealthDock’s iPhone-to-Mac sync runs strictly over Bluetooth and local Wi-Fi with encrypted device pairing—zero accounts and zero HealthDock servers. In HealthDock 1.3, the MCP server executes exclusively on your Mac (via local stdio or loopback 127.0.0.1), and HealthDock itself never uploads any health data anywhere.

Be clear on what happens next: whenever your AI tool calls an MCP function, the data returned to that AI client is sent to whichever AI provider you chose (unless you run a strictly offline local model) and is governed by that provider’s own privacy policy. Apple Health MCP Server FAQ

Is the Apple Health MCP server available in HealthDock right now?

Not yet. HealthDock 1.0 launches on the China App Store first, with other regions following afterward, and the built-in local MCP server is coming in HealthDock 1.3. If you want an AI-ready context.md summary for Claude or ChatGPT today, use our free browser-local converter right now.

Should I connect via --mcp-stdio or local Streamable HTTP?

We recommend stdio mode (/Applications/HealthDock.app/Contents/MacOS/HealthDock --mcp-stdio) whenever your AI client supports local command servers, since your client starts the binary directly without opening a port. For clients that need HTTP, HealthDock 1.3 provides http://127.0.0.1:47321/mcp with an access token, bound strictly to 127.0.0.1.

Can Claude Code or Codex modify my Apple Health records through MCP?

Never. HealthDock on iPhone requests read-only access to Apple Health. In HealthDock 1.3, MCP write tools (log_event, delete_event, set_goal, deactivate_goal) are turned off by default in Settings. Even when enabled, they only save personal life events and goals into events/YYYY-MM.jsonl and goals.json in your Mac archive folder and never write back to Apple Health.

How does HealthDock prevent double-counted sleep if I wear both an Apple Watch and a smart ring?

When multiple wearables write sleep intervals to Apple Health, HealthDock 1.3 evaluates each night using a single primary sleep source rather than stacking overlapping totals. You can adjust your preferred primary source in Settings.

Does a local MCP server mean my health data never reaches cloud AI providers?

HealthDock itself runs no servers and never uploads your data anywhere, and the MCP server runs strictly on your Mac. However, whenever an AI client like Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or Claude Desktop calls an MCP tool, the query results returned to that client are sent to whichever AI service provider powers your model and are governed by that provider’s privacy policy.
